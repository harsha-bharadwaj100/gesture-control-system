Abstract—Current real-time surface electromyography
(sEMG) gesture recognition systems heavily rely on high-density
sensor arrays or computationally expensive deep learning
models, limiting their viability for affordable, edge-device
deployment. Single-channel, AC-coupled amplifiers offer a
low-cost alternative but suffer from severe spatial ambiguity and
signal cancellation during complex motor tasks. In this paper, we
propose a novel, intent-driven Brain-Computer Interface (BCI)
architecture that overcomes single-channel hardware constraints
through an “anatomical hack”—utilizing horizontal flexor
placement to capture polarity-sensitive fine motor actuations. To
ensure real-world robustness across varying skin impedances,
we introduce a Zero-Shot Guided Onboarding sequence for
dynamic data standardization, alongside a 600ms Reaction
Time Purge to eliminate human latency from training sets.
The system achieves a raw cross-subject statistical accuracy
of 75.46%, which is elevated to high functional stability via
a rolling temporal majority-vote algorithm. Crucially, the
entire digital signal processing and XGBoost inference pipeline
operates with a latency of 9.46 ms per frame, demonstrating a
highly efficient, deployable framework for real-time prosthetic
and virtual interface control.

I. INTRODUCTION
The translation of raw human biosignals into reliable digital
commands remains a primary challenge in the development
of Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs) and accessible pros-
thetics. While laboratory-grade solutions frequently utilize
High-Density surface Electromyography (HD-sEMG) arrays
paired with Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to achieve
high classification accuracy, these systems are fundamentally
constrained by high inference latency, prohibitive costs, and
computational overhead unsuitable for low-power edge micro-
controllers.
Conversely, utilizing low-cost, single-channel AC-coupled
amplifiers presents a severe physical limitation: the inability to
spatially isolate distinct muscle bellies. This results in differen-
tial signal cancellation, making fine motor classification highly
ambiguous. Traditional machine learning models trained in
static laboratory conditions frequently degrade in real-world
deployments due to user-to-user variance in muscle density,
skin impedance, and hardware placement.
To bridge the gap between low-cost hardware and
enterprise-grade deployment, this research proposes a highly
optimized, dual-model sEMG classification architecture. The
primary objectives of this study are:
1) To demonstrate an “anatomical hack” leveraging hor-
izontal flexor sensor placement to achieve fine-motor
resolution on a single AC-coupled channel.
2) To implement a Zero-Shot Guided Onboarding calibra-
tion sequence that mathematically standardizes dynamic
user environments in under 10 seconds.
3) To drastically reduce computational latency (<10 ms per
frame) while maintaining functional UI stability through
temporal smoothing algorithms.
4) To mathematically quantify the detrimental impact of
human reaction-time latency on sEMG training datasets
via a systematic ablation study.
II. RELATEDWORK
Current research trajectories in sEMG-based gesture recog-
nition generally bifurcate into two domains: high-density ar-
rays (HD-sEMG) leveraging deep learning for maximum accu-
racy, and single-channel configurations optimized for power-
efficient edge deployment.

A. HD-sEMG and Deep Learning Latency
Recent academic work has increasingly shifted toward deep
learning frameworks to handle the spatial complexity of HD-
sEMG data. The CT-HGR framework utilizes a Vision Trans-
former (ViT) network with attention mechanisms to achieve
89.13% accuracy on instantaneous frames [2]. Similarly,
lightweight architectures like All-ConvNet attempt to simplify
feature extraction [3]. However, processing high-dimensional
data at high sampling rates often results in latency exceeding
the 10ms threshold desired for fluid user interface interactions.
The emergence of foundation models, such as the generalized
neuromotor interface by Kaifosh et al. (2025), offers out-of-
the-box generalization but relies on immense computational
infrastructure [1]. In contrast, our work maximizes the in-
formation density of a single channel, achieving functional
stability on significantly lower-power edge hardware.
B. Single-Channel Constraints and Spatial Ambiguity
Single-channel sEMG systems are preferred for wearable
BCIs due to their minimal footprint and cost. The primary
limitation of these systems is “crosstalk”—the superposition
of signals from multiple muscle groups leading to spatial
ambiguity [4]. Research by Wu et al. explored sEMG envelope
signals for single-channel gesture recognition but achieved
suboptimal recognition rates for multi-finger gestures due
to this spatial filter bottleneck [4]. Our “Anatomical Hack”
directly addresses this by forcing electrical polarities to bypass
traditional bipolar cancellation.
C. Real-Time Calibration and Temporal Stability
A persistent hurdle in deploying sEMG-BCIs is the non-
stationarity of the signal caused by fluctuating skin impedance
and sweat [6]. While hybrid frameworks modulate classifier
weights dynamically [5], they still require burdensome labeled
data collection for onboarding. By implementing zero-shot sta-
tistical standardization, our architecture ensures stable steady-
state classification without heavy retraining burdens [7].
III. HARDWARECONSTRAINTS ANDPHYSIOLOGICAL
ADAPTATIONS
A. Limitations of Single-Channel AC-Coupled Amplification
To prioritize affordability, this architecture utilizes a single-
channel, AC-coupled differential amplifier (Anthriq/Nexstem
specification). While highly efficient, AC-coupled hardware
introduces a severe physical constraint: the system strips all
direct current (DC) voltage offsets, forcibly centering the
waveform at 0.00V .
Consequently, the differential reading suffers from spatial
ambiguity. During complex multi-finger gestures, the co-
contraction of adjacent muscle groups leads to destructive
signal interference. A traditional machine learning model
relying purely on signal amplitude cannot reliably differentiate
between the actuation of an index finger and a thumb when
both generate overlapping microvolt bursts on a single axis.
B. The “Anatomical Hack” for Spatial Resolution
To overcome this hardware limitation, we implemented a
physiological adaptation for electrode placement. Rather than
utilizing standard longitudinal placement along the muscle
belly, the electrodes were oriented horizontally across the
flexor digitorum superficialis and the flexor pollicis longus.
By placing the positive terminal on the radial aspect (ori-
ented toward the thumb) and the negative terminal on the ulnar
aspect (oriented toward the index and middle fingers), the
system physically forces isolated finger actuations to produce
opposing electrical polarities. Flexing the thumb draws the
differential voltage into a positive skew, while flexing the
index finger draws it into a negative skew. This strategy
allows the classification algorithms to rely on polarity-sensitive
mathematical features.

V. DYNAMICFEATUREENGINEERING
A. The Reaction-Time Purge
When a user is visually prompted to perform a gesture,
there is a physiological latency before steady-state motor unit
recruitment occurs [8]. Our pipeline enforces a strict 600ms
“Reaction-Time Purge.” By programmatically slicing the first
600ms from every recorded gesture block, the XGBoost model
trains exclusively on the deterministic, peak-contraction phase.

ations in skin hydration, we implemented a Zero-Shot Guided
Onboarding sequence. The system dynamically calculates the
unique Mean (µ) and Standard Deviation (σ) of the user’s
current microvolt range during a 3-second baseline phase.

compiled across four distinct subjects, balancing gross and

VIII. DISCUSSION ANDLIMITATIONS
While the proposed architecture successfully bridges the gap
between low-cost hardware and functional stability, certain
limitations remain inherent to single-channel systems. First,
the horizontal flexor placement requires a high degree of pre-
cision; anatomical differences between users mean the “sweet
spot” for generating opposing polarities can vary by several
millimeters. Secondly, while the Zero-Shot Guided Onboard-
ing successfully mitigates initial skin impedance variations,
prolonged continuous usage (exceeding 60 minutes) introduces
signal drift due to sweat accumulation and muscle fatigue,
which a static 3-second initialization cannot recursively cor-
rect. Future iterations of this work will explore adaptive
algorithms capable of recalibratingµandσdynamically in
the background.
IX. CONCLUSION
By combining horizontal flexor placement with polarity-
sensitive feature extraction, dynamic zero-shot calibration,
and temporal smoothing, we have demonstrated that single-
channel, AC-coupled sEMG sensors can achieve highly stable
intent recognition. This sub-10ms architecture overcomes the
spatial ambiguity of differential amplifiers, paving the way
for seamless integration with low-cost haptic prosthetics and
edge-deployed virtual interfaces.

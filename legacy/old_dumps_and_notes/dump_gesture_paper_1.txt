Abstract—Current gesture recognition systems that use real-
time surface electromyography (sEMG) need either high-density
sensor arrays or deep learning systems that require extensive
computational resources, which restricts their ability to func-
tion in budget-friendly edge-device environments. The single-
channel AC-coupled amplifiers provide an affordable solution,
but the system faces major problems because it cannot determine
user location and it experiences signal loss during complex
motor activities. We introduce an innovative Brain-Computer
Interface (BCI) system which uses an ”anatomical hack” to
break single-channel hardware limitations by using horizontal
flexor placement to detect fine motor movements that require
polarity detection. The Zero-Shot Guided Onboarding sequence
establishes dynamic data standardization, which enables our
system to maintain its real-world performance across different
skin impedances while the 600ms Reaction Time Purge removes
human delay from training data. The system achieves a base
cross-subject statistical accuracy of 75.46%, which reaches high
functional stability through the rolling temporal majority-vote
system. The entire digital signal processing system together with
the XGBoost inference pipeline process each frame with a 9.46
millisecond delay, which proves the system can operate efficiently
as a real-time control solution for prosthetic devices and virtual
interfaces.

I. INTRODUCTION
The translation of raw human biosignals into reliable digital
commands is a major challenge in developing Brain-Computer
Interfaces (BCIs) and accessible prosthetics. Laboratory-grade
solutions often use High-Density surface Electromyography
(HD-sEMG) arrays along with Convolutional Neural Networks
(CNNs) to achieve high classification accuracy. However, these
systems face limitations due to high inference latency, high
costs, and heavy computational demands that are not suitable
for low-power edge microcontrollers.
On the other hand, using low-cost, single-channel AC-
coupled amplifiers has a significant drawback: they cannot
spatially isolate distinct muscle bellies. This lack of separation
leads to signal cancellation, which makes it difficult to classify
fine motor actions, such as telling the difference between an
index finger twitch and a thumb twitch. Additionally, tradi-
tional machine learning models that are trained in controlled
laboratory settings often perform poorly in real-world situa-
tions. This poor performance arises from differences between
users, such as variations in muscle density, skin impedance,
and where the hardware is placed.
To address the gap between low-cost hardware and high-
quality deployment, this research suggests a highly optimized,
dual-model sEMG classification architecture. The main goals
of this study are:
1) To show an ”anatomical hack,” we use horizontal flexor
sensor placement and polarity-sensitive feature extrac-
tion, including kurtosis and skewness, to gain fine-motor
resolution on a single AC-coupled channel. create a
Zero-Shot Guided Onboarding calibration sequence that
mathematically standardizes dynamic user environments
in less than 10 seconds, we will remove the need for
strict, pre-trained baselines.
2) To cut down on computational latency by a lot (less
than 10 ms per frame) while keeping the UI stable with
temporal smoothing algorithms.
3) To systematically quantify the adverse effects of human
reaction-time latency on sEMG training datasets through
a methodical ablation study.
II. RELATEDWORK
Current research trajectories in sEMG-based gesture recog-
nition generally bifurcate into two domains: high-density ar-
rays (HD-sEMG) leveraging deep learning for maximum accu-
racy, and single-channel configurations optimized for power-
efficient edge deployment.
A. HD-sEMG and the Computational Latency of Deep Learn-
ing
Recent academic work has increasingly shifted toward
deep learning frameworks to handle the spatial complexity
of HD-sEMG data. The CT-HGR framework utilizes a Vi-
sion Transformer (ViT) network with attention mechanisms
to achieve 89.13% accuracy on instantaneous frames [2].

Similarly, lightweight architectures like All-ConvNet attempt
to simplify feature extraction [3]. However, processing high-
dimensional data at high sampling rates often results in latency
exceeding the 10ms threshold desired for fluid user interface
(UI) interactions.
The most significant recent development is the emergence
of sEMG foundation models. Kaifosh et al. (2025) introduced
a generic neuromotor interface trained on over 197 hours of
multi-user data [1], [4]. While groundbreaking for out-of-the-
box generalization, this approach relies on immense compu-
tational infrastructure. In contrast to massive, data-intensive
paradigms, our work maximizes the information density of a
single channel, achieving functional stability on significantly
lower-power edge hardware.
B. Single-Channel Constraints and Spatial Ambiguity
Single-channel sEMG systems are preferred for wearable
BCIs due to their minimal footprint and cost. The primary
limitation of these systems is “crosstalk”—the superposition
of signals from multiple muscle groups leading to spatial
ambiguity [5]. Research by Wu et al. (2018) explored sEMG
envelope signals for single-channel gesture recognition but
achieved suboptimal recognition rates for multi-finger gestures
due to this spatial filter bottleneck [5]. Our “Anatomical Hack”
directly addresses this by forcing electrical polarities to bypass
traditional bipolar cancellation.
C. Real-Time Calibration and Temporal Stability
A persistent hurdle in deploying sEMG-BCIs is the non-
stationarity of the signal caused by fluctuating skin impedance
and sweat [7]. While hybrid frameworks modulate classifier
weights dynamically [6], they still require burdensome labeled
data collection for onboarding. Furthermore, functional BCI
accuracy is highly dependent on temporal stability. Physio-
logically, the initial transient phase of a voluntary contraction
contains highly stochastic bursts [9]. By purging this high-
variance transient data, alongside implementing zero-shot sta-
tistical standardization, our architecture ensures stable steady-
state classification without heavy retraining burdens [8].
III. HARDWARECONSTRAINTS ANDPHYSIOLOGICAL
ADAPTATIONS
A. Limitations of Single-Channel AC-Coupled Amplifi-
cation The deployment of accessible BCIs is often bottle-
necked by the high cost and computational demands of High-
Density sEMG arrays. To prioritize affordability and edge-
device compatibility, this architecture utilizes a single-channel,
AC-coupled differential amplifier. While highly efficient, AC-
coupled hardware introduces a severe physical constraint: the
system strips all direct current (DC) voltage offsets, forcibly
centering the waveform at 0.00V .
Consequently, the differential reading (measured between
the positive and negative terminals) suffers from spatial ambi-
guity. During complex multi-finger gestures, the co-contraction
of adjacent muscle groups leads to destructive signal interfer-
ence. A traditional machine learning model relying purely on
signal amplitude or spectral density cannot reliably differen-
tiate between the actuation of an index finger and a thumb
when both generate overlapping microvolt bursts on a single
axis.
B. The ”Anatomical Hack” for Spatial Resolution To over-
come this hardware limitation without introducing additional
sensory channels, we implemented a physiological adaptation
for electrode placement. Rather than utilizing standard longi-
tudinal placement along the muscle belly, the electrodes were
oriented horizontally across the flexor digitorum superficialis
and the flexor pollicis longus.
By placing the positive terminal on the radial aspect (ori-
ented toward the thumb) and the negative terminal on the ulnar
aspect (oriented toward the index and middle fingers), the
system physically forces isolated finger actuations to produce
opposing electrical polarities.
Radial Actuation: Flexing the thumb draws the differential
voltage into a positive skew.
Ulnar Actuation: Flexing the index finger draws the differ-
ential voltage into a negative skew.
By actively engineering the sensor placement to lever-
age human neuroanatomy, the single AC-coupled channel is
transformed into a pseudo-spatial sensor. This physiological
strategy allows the subsequent classification algorithms to
rely on polarity-sensitive mathematical features, effectively
resolving the signal cancellation problem inherent in low-cost
amplification.

ages, the raw analog signal from the Nexstem amplifier is
routed through a rigid, dual-buffer Digital Signal Processing
(DSP) pipeline.

via an Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) notch filter (ω 0 =

V. DYNAMICFEATUREENGINEERING& ZERO-SHOT
CALIBRATION
Once the analog signal is digitally filtered into a stable,
noise-free representation of muscle effort, the system extracts
high-dimensional mathematical features to feed the XGBoost
classifiers.
A. The Reaction-Time Purge
A fundamental flaw in many sEMG datasets is the inclu-
sion of human cognitive and electromechanical delay (EMD).
When a user is visually prompted to perform a gesture, there is
a physiological latency of 300ms to 500ms before steady-state
motor unit recruitment occurs.
Including this transient phase in training data introduces
severe label noise, as the classifier is effectively trained on
“Rest” data labeled as an active gesture. To rectify this, our
pipeline enforces a strict 600ms “Reaction-Time Purge.” By
programmatically slicing the first 600ms from every recorded
gesture block, the XGBoost model trains exclusively on the
deterministic, peak-contraction phase of the MUAP firing
pattern.
B. Feature Space Separation
Because the architecture relies on two distinct models
to bypass single-channel constraints, the feature extraction
pipelines were bifurcated:
•Gross Motor Model (Polarity-Invariant):To ensure
the classification of macro-movements (Fist, Open Hand)
remains immune to accidental electrode shifting or re-
versed wiring, 13 strictly polarity-invariant features were
utilized. These included Mean Absolute Value (MA V),
Variance, Waveform Length, Zero Crossings, and Slope
Sign Changes.
•Fine Motor Model (Polarity-Sensitive):Because the
horizontal flexor “Anatomical Hack” forces opposite elec-
trical polarities for the index finger and thumb, 3 polarity-
sensitive features were reintroduced to the base 13 fea-
tures: Raw Mean, Skewness, and Kurtosis.
C. Zero-Shot Guided Onboarding
Traditional subject-optimized models suffer from severe per-
formance degradation across different sessions due to fluctua-
tions in skin hydration and impedance. To achieve immediate
commercial viability, we implemented a Zero-Shot Guided
Onboarding sequence.
Prior to real-time inference, the user holds a sequence
of baseline gestures for 3 seconds. The system dynamically
calculates the unique Mean (µ) and Standard Deviation (σ)
of the user’s current microvolt range. By applying Z-score
standardization locally before feeding the 300ms window to
the global model, the live signal is perfectly mapped to the
normalized mathematical space of the training environment,
entirely eliminating the need for heavy, supervised retraining.
VI. EXPERIMENTALSETUP ANDABLATIONSTUDY
To rigorously validate the proposed architecture, an ablation
study was conducted on a custom dataset compiled across four
distinct subjects.
A. Dataset Compilation and Model Hyperparameters
The dataset comprised 9,023 continuous sliding windows
(300ms window size, 1000Hz sampling rate) compiled across
16 recording sessions. The data thoroughly captured user-
to-user variance in muscle density and baseline impedance.
The underlying classification was performed using eXtreme
Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) classifiers, optimized with a
maximum tree depth of 12 and 400 estimators to capture non-
linear feature interactions rapidly.
B. Ablation Study Results
The Gross Motor model achieved a baseline cross-subject
statistical accuracy of 75.46%. While this represents a high
benchmark for single-channel, zero-shot calibrated systems,
the ablation study (Table I) reveals the critical importance of
the proposed DSP and data-cleaning methodologies.

Notably, omitting the 600ms Reaction-Time Purge caused
accuracy to plummet by nearly 10%, mathematically proving
that human cognitive latency poisons sEMG training datasets.
Furthermore, an isolated ablation test on the Fine Motor
model validated the Anatomical Hack. When the polarity-
sensitive features (Skewness and Kurtosis) were stripped from
the 16-feature array, the fine-motor accuracy degraded from its
functional baseline down to 36.28%—functionally equivalent
to random guessing for a 3-class target. This confirms that
without polarity-sensitive metrics, single-channel AC-coupled
amplifiers cannot resolve spatial fine-motor ambiguity.

VII. CONCLUSION
The combination of horizontal flexor placement with
polarity-sensitive feature extraction together with dynamic
zero-shot calibration and temporal smoothing methods shows
that single-channel AC-coupled sEMG sensors can achieve sta-
ble intent recognition performance. The sub-10ms architecture
enables users to deploy BCI systems with both cost-effective
hardware and dependable performance.

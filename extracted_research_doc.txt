EMG Gesture Recognition: Research Paper Draft (Sections 1-9)
Project Information
Project Title: Single-Channel Surface EMG Gesture Recognition Using Generalized Triple Ensemble Modeling Objective: To develop a robust, generalized machine learning pipeline capable of classifying five distinct hand gestures across multiple participants using a single-channel surface EMG, overcoming inter-participant baseline variance and significant mains hum noise.
Participant Information
Data was collected from a diverse group of 15 participants to ensure the generalized model was not overfit to a specific physiological profile.
Demographic Breakdown: - Total Participants: 15 - Gender Distribution: 9 Female, 6 Male
- Age Distribution: - 13 participants in the 20–21 years age range. - 2 participants in older demographics (one 30–40 years, one 40–50 years).
(Note: Medical history and dominant hand data were not explicitly tracked for this dataset. All participant identities have been anonymized for the purpose of this study).
Data Collection Protocol
The data collection phase was strictly controlled to ensure standardization across the 15 datasets.
Hardware Setup: - Device: National Instruments Data Acquisition (NI-DAQ) system (Dev1/ai0). - Sensor: Single-channel surface EMG (sEMG) electrode. - Sampling Frequency: 1,000 Hz.
Recording Protocol: - Target Gestures (5): Fist, Open Palm, Pinch, Wrist Up, Wrist Down. - Cycle Timing: Each repetition consisted of a 4-second active muscle contraction followed by a 4-second complete rest period. - Repetitions: Participants were instructed to perform 30 to 50 repetitions per gesture. - Total Dataset Volume: A total of 3,994 valid active gesture sequences were successfully captured across the 15 participants, resulting in roughly 20 minutes of recording time per subject.
Preprocessing and Signal Conditioning Pipeline
To process raw single-channel sEMG signals across diverse participants, an advanced signal conditioning pipeline was engineered. This pipeline addresses two main challenges: high-amplitude 50 Hz power line noise (and its high-frequency harmonics) and user contraction latency.
Architectural Design: Cumulative Sequential vs. Independent Processing
A critical design decision in this pipeline is the use of cumulative sequential (layer-by-layer) processing rather than independent parallel filters.
To demonstrate the necessity of this sequential architecture, we compare the cumulative filtering steps (Figure 1) against the outcome of applying each filter independently directly to the raw, noisy input (Figure 2).

Figure 2: Independent Preprocessing Application (Failed Parallel Method)
sEMG Preprocessing Applied Independently Mathematical & Analytical Comparison:
Harmonic Notch Filter (Stage 1):
Cumulative: Eliminates the 50 Hz mains hum and its higher-order harmonics, revealing the underlying low-amplitude muscle burst shapes.
Independent: While it removes the electrical hum, the baseline drift and low-frequency motion artifacts remain, preventing clean window segmenting.
Bandpass Filter (Stage 2):
Cumulative (Applied to Notched): Eliminates high-frequency sensor noise and low-frequency motion artifacts, standardizing the signal to the active 20 Hz - 450 Hz sEMG band.
Independent (Applied to Raw): Fails completely because the massive 50 Hz mains hum falls directly inside the passband, leaving the periodic hum untouched.
Teager-Kaiser Energy Operator (TKEO) (Stage 3):
Cumulative (Applied to Bandpassed): Accentuates the instant amplitude and frequency spikes of active muscle action potentials: [ = x^2(t) - x(t-1) x(t+1) ] while suppressing remaining background white noise.
Independent (Applied to Raw): The TKEO amplifies high-frequency energy. Applying it to raw data magnifies the high-amplitude 50 Hz mains hum and high-frequency noise spikes, completely drowning out the actual muscle contraction signal.
Moving Average Envelope (Stage 4):
Cumulative (Applied to TKEO): A 200ms moving average envelope over the absolute TKEO signal yields a clean, smooth activation profile. A 1.5-second sliding window dynamically harvests the maximum energy contraction window.
Independent (Applied to Raw): Fails because the envelope simply tracks the continuous, high-amplitude 50 Hz noise offset, resulting in a flat envelope from which no active gesture bursts can be extracted.

Generalization Strategy
The major bottleneck in building a unified classification model across multiple participants is the physiological baseline variance. Because of differing skin impedance, subcutaneous fat thickness, and electrode placement geometry, a raw sEMG voltage value of 0.5V during a “Fist” gesture for one participant could equal a “Pinch” gesture or even a “Rest” baseline for another.
To overcome this without utilizing complex, slow deep-learning domain adaptation, we implemented a Per-Participant Feature Standardization technique. Before pooling the feature matrices for model training, each individual participant’s feature matrix X(p) was standardized

Machine Learning Models
We benchmarked 4 classical and deep learning architectures against a proposed ensemble classifier to determine the optimal generalized mapping for single-channel sEMG signals:
Support Vector Machine (SVM): Implemented with a Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel, optimized for separating high-dimensional feature bounds.
K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN): A distance-based instance classifier (k=5), acting as a baseline for local feature proximity.
1D-CNN + LSTM Deep Learning Network: Composed of two Conv1D layers (64 and 128 filters, kernel sizes 10 and 5) for temporal sequence extraction, coupled with a 64-unit LSTM block to process the raw voltage sequences directly.
Random Forest (RF): A bagging ensemble of 500 decision trees to evaluate ensemble partition boundaries.
Proposed Triple Ensemble Voting Classifier: A soft-voting ensemble combining XGBoost (n_estimators=500, max_depth=15), Random Forest (n_estimators=500), and HistGradientBoosting (max_iter=500) to leverage gradient boosted trees and bagging trees simultaneously.

Live Hardware Inference Deployment
To validate the practical utility of the proposed system, the trained model was deployed as a real-time gesture control interface (live_advanced_inference.py) hooked to the physical NI-DAQ hardware.
Dynamic Live Calibration Protocol
Because the generalized model relies on Per-Participant Standardization, the live inference script must establish the user’s specific muscle baseline. To do this, a guided 6-stage live calibration loop was developed: 1. Baseline Rest (5s): The user relaxes their arm. The script records the baseline mean and noise. 2. Gesture Cycling (2s per gesture): The system prompts the user to perform and hold each of the five gestures sequentially (Fist, Open Palm, Pinch, Wrist Up, Wrist Down).
The calibration data is filtered and passed to the feature extraction block. The resulting feature mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) are saved as the user’s personal scaling profile.
Real-Time Processing Loop
Once calibrated, the system streams data at 1000 Hz: - Windowing: A 1.5-second rolling buffer is filled. - Processing: Every 200ms (5 times a second), the buffer is notch filtered, bandpass filtered, and its 16 features are extracted. - Normalization: The features are standardized using the user’s calibration profile: (x−μ)/σ. - Voting: The standardized vector is fed to the pre-loaded ensemble model. - Smoothing Queue: A 5-sample history queue outputs the mode prediction to prevent visual jitter, resulting in a live prediction latency of less than 200ms.
